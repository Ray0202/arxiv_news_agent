现有 inference engine 把 decode 结束当成请求终点，直接 end-of-turn eviction。对 chat 这还行，但 agent 在 tool call 之间会暂停；一旦 KV cache 被踢掉，下一轮不是重算 prefill，就是从 CPU reload，还会在 GPU 队列里再等一次。InferCept 这类方法只算 reload cost，没把多轮里累积的 per-turn queueing delay 算进去，也扛不住 tool 时长抖动。

Continnum 的关键动作是给 KV cache 加 TTL。对每个会触发 tool call 的 request $r$，它把保留代价写成 $\mathsf{Cost}(\tau,r)$，把收益拆成 $\mathsf{CacheMissCost}(r)$ 和 $\mathsf{OutofOrderCost}(r)$，再用历史 tool-call duration 的 empirical CDF 选出让期望净收益最大的 $\tau^*$。调度器再叠加 program-level FCFS，按 preempted、TTL 是否有效、program arrival 三层排序，过期、终止或死锁时自动 unpin。

实验用 100 条 SWE-Bench mini-swe-agent trace、100 条 BFCL V4 Web Search trace，以及 Company A 的 500 个 SWE-Bench-Verified 任务，在 Llama-3.1-8B/70B、Gemma-3-12B 和 GLM-4.5-355B 上回放。Continnum 在多轮 agent 工作负载上把平均 delay 改善 1.12x-3.66x，并把 throughput 提高 1.10x-3.22x；在内部 SWE-agent 测试里，delay 最多降 8.18x。

它的价值在于把 KV cache retention 从“要不要保”变成“保多久”，而且额外调度开销只有毫秒级。边界也清楚：方法主要验证了 ReAct 式、顺序的 tool-interleaving agent；对 speculative branching、异步 multi-agent 协调和 context folding 还没覆盖。TTL 估计还依赖历史分布，冷启动和分布漂移时会变钝。

Most inference engines treat decoding as the end of a request and evict its KV cache immediately. That works for chat, but not for agentic loops: tool calls create pauses, and once the cache is gone the next turn either recomputes prefill or reloads from CPU, then waits again behind unrelated GPU work. InferCept-like policies only price in reload cost; they miss the queueing bubble that accumulates over turns and they assume tool-call durations are predictable enough to preserve safely.

Continnum's move is to make KV retention a TTL decision. For each request $r$ that triggers a tool call, it prices the pinning cost as $\mathsf{Cost}(\tau,r)$ and the benefit as saved $\mathsf{CacheMissCost}(r)$ plus avoided $\mathsf{OutofOrderCost}(r)$, then chooses $\tau^*$ by maximizing expected net benefit under the empirical CDF of historical tool-call durations. The scheduler layers a TTL-aware priority on top of program-level FCFS, and it unpins on expiry, program termination, or deadlock recovery.

On 100 mini-swe-agent traces from SWE-Bench, 100 BFCL V4 Web Search traces, and 500 SWE-Bench-Verified tasks in Company A's internal testbed, the system is consistent. Across three hardware/model setups it cuts average delay by 1.12x to 3.66x and lifts throughput by 1.10x to 3.22x. In the internal SWE-agent deployment, delay drops by up to 8.18x.

The practical win is that it turns KV cache retention from a binary keep-or-evict choice into a timed one. The limit is also clear: the design is validated on sequential ReAct-style tool-interleaving agents, not speculative branches or asynchronous multi-agent control flow. Its TTL estimate is only as good as the historical duration distribution, so cold start and distribution shift remain weak points.

LLM agent 一碰时间序列就分裂：把数值直接串成文本，numerical hallucination 和算不准一起出现；只让 TSFM 看原始序列，又抓不到文本里的语义与未来形态。于是，跨域零样本预测要么只有解释、没有精度，要么只有精度、没有可读性。

关键动作是把同一个系统拆成两个角色：LLM reasoner 先借助 $23$ 个统计 tools，从历史序列和文本上下文里抽出形态描述 $r$；再由 Kairos TSFM 把 $r$ 编码成 semantic prior，通过 gated cross-attention 融进 decoder。训练上再配 T-STAR：$40k$+高质轨迹，来自 29 个 datasets、9 个 domains，30% 做了 metadata masking；RL 不只看终局，而是按 turn 给 credit。

实验在 Time-MMD 和 Time-IMM 上做零样本评测。Time-MMD 里，KairosAgent 在 $8$ 个域中拿下 $6$ 个域的最佳 MSE/MAE；例如 Agriculture MSE 0.194，对 ChronosBolt 0.218，Traffic MSE 0.151，对 Aurora 0.162。Reasoning 方面，turn-level RL 把 Energy 的 morphology accuracy 提到 50.47%，高于 SFT-only 的 45.21% 和 outcome-level RL 的 43.33%。

这说明语义先验不是装饰，而是能直接改善 forecasting 的有效输入；工具调用也不是固定模板，而是会按 trend、volatility、extrema 等统计特征分层选择。局限也明确：本文只在 Kairos 这一个 TSFM 底座上验证，尚未证明可无缝迁移到别的 TSFM；而且只评估 reasoning 和 forecasting，没有覆盖 classification 或 anomaly detection。

LLM agents break on time series in two different ways. If you serialize numbers as text, the model loses numeric fidelity and starts hallucinating. If you keep the forecaster purely numerical, it never sees the semantic cues that often explain what the future should look like. The result is a familiar trade-off: explanation without accuracy, or accuracy without an explanation.

The trick is to split the system into two jobs. An LLM reasoner uses 23 statistical tools to turn the past sequence and optional text context into a morphology description $r$. A Kairos forecaster then encodes $r$ as a semantic prior and injects it through gated cross-attention into the decoder. Training is staged too: SFT on T-STAR trajectories, multimodal alignment for the forecaster, then GRPO with turn-level credit assignment instead of a single terminal reward.

On Time-MMD and Time-IMM, the paper reports strong zero-shot forecasting. On Time-MMD, KairosAgent is best on MSE/MAE in 6 of 8 domains; for example, Agriculture reaches 0.194 MSE versus ChronosBolt at 0.218, and Traffic reaches 0.151 versus Aurora at 0.162. For reasoning, turn-level RL lifts Energy morphology accuracy to 50.47%, above SFT-only at 45.21% and outcome-level RL at 43.33%.

The main takeaway is that semantic priors can be a real input to forecasting, not just a natural-language explanation layered on top. The tool traces also show a nontrivial policy: early turns focus on trend and volatility, later turns on extrema, distribution, and frequency. The weak spot is scope. The backbone is only Kairos, so the claimed architecture agnosticism is not yet demonstrated, and the evaluation stays on reasoning and forecasting rather than other time-series tasks.

In multi-agent systems, communication does not automatically improve collective judgment. It can also create herding, polarization, and bias amplification, especially when the task is subjective. The problem is not that these effects are unknown; it is that they are hard to compress into a single predictive dynamics.

The trick is to treat agent interaction as a statistical-mechanics process: agents stochastically favor states with lower social pressure and repeatedly revise their opinions. With only initial opinions, the model captures individual trajectories and reduces group behavior to three regimes: indifference, polarization, and consensus.

The authors test this on more than 10,000 communities of language-model agents, spanning objective mathematics questions and subjective political statements. They report that the model predicts individual trajectories, beats all standard baselines, generalizes to unseen community graphs, and reproduces the observed distribution of group archetypes. The fitted parameters further suggest that communities operate below the critical social temperature, attractive ties outweigh repulsive ones, and agents holding the correct answer exert the strongest pull.

The main contribution is a compact dynamical law for collective agent behavior, not just another benchmark win. But the evidence here is abstract-only, so the exact baselines, evaluation protocol, and robustness checks are still opaque. It is relevant to multi-agent communication and bias, though the leap from opinion communities to tool-using or planning agents remains untested.

Current tool menus rank by request relevance, so they often expose the final action while hiding the upstream tools that produce its inputs. For multi-step tasks, that means the agent sees an attractive endpoint but not a runnable path.

The move here is to treat the menu as an execution prior over a state path: a pre-execution route from the observable request state to the desired outcome. The encoder represents which tools can run from the current state, how outputs satisfy later inputs, and which orderings recur in training paths. A retriever covers an executable entry, the missing-input producers, and the final action; a reranker then places producers before consumers.

On ToolBench, online success rises from 0.737 to 0.898. The abstract also says the method beats retrieval, reranking, generation, and routing baselines without changing the agent, and that a 32-tool State-Path menu covers more complete chains than the official 128-tool list. The gain persists across executor families with different model capacities.

The result is a clearer diagnosis of where online agents fail: not just tool selection, but execution order. The evidence is promising, but with only the abstract I cannot check how paths were labeled, how menu size affects recall, or whether the same pattern holds in longer-horizon or larger-library settings.

异步训练语言模型时，policy lag 会把 critic 锁在过时的 behavior policy 上，价值估计随之偏移。现有异步 LLM 方法主要修 actor，不处理这个偏差；经典 off-policy value correction 又很难搬到长程 agent 任务：修正窗太短，回报进不来；太长，importance ratios 会随轨迹长度指数漂移。

BRACE 的关键动作，是把 Bellman-residual correction 限在 policy token 的一个前缀里，再把后半段接到一个常数权重的 Monte-Carlo tail 上。这样做把“纠偏”和“传播奖励”拆开：前缀负责对齐当前策略，尾部负责把真实回报送回去，避免长轨迹上比率连乘失控。

摘要给出的实验结果是，BRACE 在 BrowseComp-Plus 上的 mean@1 比最强 baseline 高 $2.4\%$，每步比 synchronous training 快 $2.46\times$，并且在离策略 $50$ 次更新时仍保持稳定。

这篇工作的价值在于，它不是继续修 actor，而是直接处理 asynchronous RL 里 stale critic 的病灶。限制也很清楚：目前只看到摘要，稳定性和收益都只在 BrowseComp-Plus 这一个任务族上被点到，具体的 horizon、anchor 设定和消融都还看不到。

Asynchronous training makes policy lag the norm, and that lag biases the critic toward a stale behavior policy. Existing asynchronous LLM methods mostly fix the actor and leave this value error intact. Classical off-policy correction also breaks down on long-horizon agent tasks: a short correction window fails to propagate reward, while a long one makes importance ratios drift exponentially.

BRACE’s move is to cap Bellman-residual correction at a prefix of policy tokens and attach a constant-weight Monte Carlo tail after that. The prefix handles policy alignment; the tail carries reward propagation. The point is to separate correction from return transport so the ratio products do not explode over long trajectories.

In the abstract’s reported results, BRACE improves mean@1 on BrowseComp-Plus by $2.4\%$ over the strongest baseline. It also runs $2.46\times$ faster per step than synchronous training and stays stable $50$ updates off-policy.

The paper’s contribution is most relevant if you care about asynchronous RL for LLM agents, especially when critic staleness is the bottleneck rather than actor optimization. But this is abstract-only evidence: the claim is currently anchored to one benchmark, and the actual horizon choices, anchoring details, and ablations are not visible here.

Persistent agents keep running into a bad split: the fact they should remember is not always the fact they should use. A superseded employer record can still answer a historical question, while it should be excluded from a current-state answer. Prior memory systems mostly manage retention or retrieval, but not the distinction between stored history and answer-time evidence.

RD-Forget makes that split explicit, without training. A frozen curator reads the archive, extracts atomic facts, groups them into semantic slots, and marks same-slot replacements as superseded. A historical-intent path can rescue older entries when a query asks for the past. The answer-time view is then built as a rate-distortion problem: choose $Z^*(q)\in\arg\min_{Z\subseteq C(q)}D_q(Z)$ subject to $\sum_{m\in Z}c(m)\leq B$.

The paper tests AMB-Text, LME-KU, MAB-FC, BEAM, and PersonaMem-v1 across Qwen3.5-flash, GPT-5.6-Luna, MiniMax-M2.5, and Kimi-K2.5.
